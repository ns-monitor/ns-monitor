Glossary

Name Description
TIR (%) Time in Range. The percentage of sensor readings falling within the standard target glucose range of 3.9–10.0 mmol/L (70–180 mg/dL). Calculated as (readings in range ÷ total readings) × 100. Key consensus target is >70% for overall glycemic control. (Zone: Range)
TITR (%) Time in Tight Range. The percentage of sensor readings falling within a tighter target band of 3.9–7.8 mmol/L (70–140 mg/dL). Measures precise postprandial and baseline regulation, particularly relevant for closed-loop/automated insulin delivery (AID) systems. (Zone: Range)
TBR (%) Time Below Range. The percentage of readings below 3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL), combining Level 1 (3.0–3.8 mmol/L) and Level 2 (<3.0 mmol/L) hypoglycemia. International consensus guidelines recommend keeping total TBR under 4% (with Level 2 <1%). (Zone: Range)
TAR (%) Time Above Range. The percentage of readings above 10.0 mmol/L (180 mg/dL), combining Level 1 (10.1–13.9 mmol/L) and Level 2 (>13.9 mmol/L) hyperglycemia. Consensus guidelines recommend keeping total TAR under 25%. (Zone: Range)
GMI (%) Glucose Management Indicator. An estimate of expected lab HbA1c derived from the mean CGM glucose level across the period: GMI = 3.31 + (0.431 × mean glucose in mmol/L). Provides an indicator of overall glycemia for the selected window. (Zone: Stability)
CV (%) Coefficient of Variation. A normalized measure of glucose variability calculated as (standard deviation ÷ mean glucose) × 100. Lower values reflect greater glucose predictability; clinical consensus guidelines recommend CV ≤ 36% for stable glycemic control. (Zone: Stability)
MAG Mean Absolute Glucose. Quantifies the rate and magnitude of glucose fluctuations by summing the absolute rate of change (|ΔBG|) between consecutive readings divided by elapsed time (mmol/L/hour). Captures rapid swings and glucose volatility even when overall mean remains steady. (Zone: Stability)
LBGI Low Blood Glucose Index. A non-linear risk score (Kovatchev et al.) specifically quantifying hypoglycemia risk. Glucose values are transformed onto a progressive risk scale where lower readings receive exponential weighting, providing an early warning of severe low risk. (Zone: Risk)
HBGI High Blood Glucose Index. The hyperglycemic counterpart to LBGI, applying progressive weighting to elevated glucose readings to quantify the clinical severity and sustained burden of high glucose excursions. (Zone: Risk)
GRI Glycemia Risk Index. A composite score (0–100) that synthesizes both hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia severity into a single actionable metric: GRI = (3.0 × Hypo Component) + (1.6 × Hyper Component). Lower scores represent lower overall glycemic risk, with ≤ 20 indicating very low risk. (Zone: Risk)
Silhouette Area Normalized Polygon Silhouette Area. The geometric surface area enclosed by the 10 normalized (0–100) metric vertices on the radar chart. A larger area indicates closer alignment with ideal targets across all range, stability, and risk dimensions; the percentage change reflects overall period-over-period progress.

BG Distribution

Blood Glucose Frequency Distribution: This chart displays the frequency of your blood glucose levels in 0.1 mmol/L increments. A normal distribution centered around the target range (e.g., 5.0–7.0 mmol/L) with a narrow spread represents optimal glycemic control. A wider, right-skewed distribution indicates frequent excursions into hyperglycemia.

GRI Risk Grid

- -
The Glycemia Risk Index (GRI) is a composite, single-number score (0–100) that evaluates overall glycaemic risk by incorporating the severity and frequency of both high and low blood glucose excursions.
Traditional metrics like Time in Range (TIR) or GMI measure averages or time spent within a target zone, but they can't capture the danger of extreme fluctuations — two people can share an identical TIR while one experiences severe, life-threatening hypoglycaemia and the other stays relatively stable.
The GRI applies heavier mathematical penalties to extreme highs (>13.9 mmol/L) and severe lows (<3.0 mmol/L), condensing both into one actionable score.
X-axis (hypoglycaemia component) = V.Low + (0.8 × Low). Y-axis (hyperglycaemia component) = V.High + (0.5 × High). GRI = (3.0 × X) + (1.6 × Y). The coloured bands are the diagonal isobars where that equation crosses the 20/40/60/80 cutoffs.
