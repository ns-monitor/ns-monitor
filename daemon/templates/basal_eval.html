Modal Basal Evaluation

What is Modal Basal Evaluation?

A non-invasive clinical audit that evaluates 24-hour diurnal basal rate adequacy using observational closed-loop data, without requiring disruptive open-loop fasting days.

How It Works:

Aggregates observational data into 288 5-minute diurnal bins across the selected date range. By systematically excluding confounding meal absorption, active carbs, and rescue carbs, it isolates time windows where glucose behavior reflects underlying pump basal settings.

The 3 Synchronized Perspectives:
  • Equilibrium (Panel 1): Did the loop persistently run a net insulin surplus or deficit?
  • Actionable Delta (Panel 2): What pump rate changes are indicated, and when (accounting for the 1.5h–2h insulin depot shift)?
  • Root Cause (Panel 3): Was glucose in equilibrium, persistently dropping (sensitive), or rising (resistant)?

Exclusion Filters Purpose

Why Filter Closed-Loop Data?

Automated insulin delivery continuously doses temp basals and micro-boluses to counteract food, exercise, and glucose excursions. To accurately evaluate basal rates, confounding meal absorption and unannounced carb rises must be ring-fenced.

Balancing Purity vs Sample Size:

More aggressive filters yield cleaner fasting data but fewer valid 5-minute bins. Default settings are clinically optimized for automated systems.

Filtered Net IOB Equilibrium (Volume & Balance)

Panel 1: Net IOB Equilibrium

What It Shows:

Net IOB represents the insulin on board delivered above or below your pump's scheduled profile during clean periods. The solid blue line is the diurnal median; the blue ribbon represents the 25th–75th interquartile range (IQR).

How to Interpret:
  • Near Zero Line (Dashed): Perfect equilibrium. Scheduled basal matches physiological fasting requirements.
  • Median Above Zero (>0 U): The loop consistently had to dose extra insulin to maintain target BG. Scheduled basal is too low (under-basaled).
  • Median Below Zero (<0 U): The loop had to suspend or throttle delivery to avoid lows. Scheduled basal is too high (over-basaled).
  • Wide Shaded Ribbon: High day-to-day variability; check lifestyle, activity, or meal timing consistency.
Zero Line = Perfect Profile Equilibrium
Scheduled vs Enacted Delivery Rate (Actionable Setting Delta)

Panel 2: Scheduled vs Enacted Rates

What It Shows:
  • Light Blue Dashed Line: Baseline scheduled basal profile in your pump.
  • Dark Blue Line: Median enacted basal rate delivered by the loop.
  • Orange Line: Effective delivery rate including micro-boluses (SMBs).
The 1.5h – 2.0h Depot Shift Rule:

Crucial Clinical Rule: Subcutaneous rapid insulin takes 60–90 minutes to peak. If enacted delivery rises at 06:00 to fight dawn phenomenon, the pump profile adjustment must be programmed 1.5 to 2 hours earlier (at 04:00–04:30) so the insulin is active in the blood when the need arrives.

Depot Shift Rule: Apply adjustments 1.5h - 2h prior
Modal Deviation State Breakdown (Root Cause Distribution)

Panel 3: Deviation Composition

What It Shows:

Displays the proportion of 5-minute intervals at each time of day classified into 3 physiological deviation states:

  • Dark Gray (Neutral / EQUAL): BG matched model expectations (|deviation| < 2.0). Ideal equilibrium.
  • Red (Sensitivity / SENS): BG dropped faster or rose slower than expected. Indicates excess insulin or increased sensitivity.
  • Green (Resistance / RES): BG rose faster or dropped slower than expected. Indicates insulin resistance, dawn phenomenon, or unannounced carbs.
Diagnostic Value:

Reveals whether an abnormal Net IOB in Panel 1 is driven by persistent resistance vs volatile over-corrections.

Black: Neutral (|dev| < 2.0) | Red: SENS | Green: RES
Sample Density & Statistical Confidence

Panel 4: Sample Density & Confidence

What It Shows:

Shows the count of clean, un-excluded days that contributed data to each 5-minute diurnal bin. Bars are color-coded by statistical reliability:

  • Green (High): Robust sample size (>35% of total days). Recommendations are statistically sound.
  • Yellow (Moderate): Moderate sample (15–35%). Review alongside clinical context.
  • Red / Dark (Sparse): Low sample (<15%). Daytime hours often have low sample due to frequent meals; do not over-interpret sparse peaks.
