Glossary

Name Description
TIR (3.9-10) (%) Time in Range. The percentage of sensor readings falling within the standard target glucose range of 3.9-10.0 mmol/L (70-180 mg/dL). Calculated as (readings in range ÷ total readings) × 100. This is the headline metric recommended by the 2019 international CGM consensus guidelines for overall glycemic control.
TITR (3.9-7.8) (%) Time in Tight Range. The percentage of readings within a stricter target band of 3.9-7.8 mmol/L (70-140 mg/dL), used alongside standard TIR as a more demanding measure of control, particularly relevant for closed-loop/AID systems capable of tighter regulation.
Avg BG Average Blood Glucose. The simple arithmetic mean of all CGM glucose readings across the period, in mmol/L.
GMI (%) Glucose Management Indicator. An estimate of what an equivalent HbA1c would be, derived from mean CGM glucose rather than a blood test. Formula: GMI = 3.31 + (0.431 × mean glucose in mmol/L). It approximates but does not replace a lab HbA1c, since it reflects only the measured CGM period rather than the ~3-month red cell lifespan HbA1c averages over.
CV (%) (not MAVG) Coefficient of Variation. A normalized measure of glucose variability: (standard deviation ÷ mean) × 100. Calculated directly over the full window rather than as an average of daily values (hence "not MAVG"), since averaging daily CV values would distort the result. Consensus guidelines target CV ≤36% as an indicator of stable, predictable glucose patterns.
GVI Glycemic Variability Index. Compares the actual length of the glucose trace over time to the length of a perfectly flat line at the same average value. A ratio of 1.0 represents no variability; higher values indicate a more jagged, unstable trace. Calculated as the summed curve length of the glucose trace divided by the summed elapsed time, capturing both the frequency and magnitude of fluctuations rather than just their spread.
HBGI (High Risk) High Blood Glucose Risk Index. A weighted risk score (Kovatchev et al.) quantifying the risk contributed by high readings, giving disproportionately more weight to more extreme highs. Glucose values are first transformed onto a symmetric risk scale, then only the "high" side of that scale is summed and averaged. Unlike TIR, it captures the severity of excursions, not just their frequency.
LBGI (Low Risk) Low Blood Glucose Risk Index. The same risk-transformation method as HBGI, but summing only the "low" side of the scale, so it specifically quantifies hypoglycemia risk with more weight given to more severe lows. Considered clinically more sensitive to dangerous lows than a simple time-below-range percentage.
TDD Total Daily Dose. Total insulin delivered per day, combining both bolus (meal/correction) and basal (background) insulin, in units.
Carbs Carbohydrates. Total grams of carbohydrate logged per day, from meal entries.
V.Low (<3) (%) Very Low. Percentage of time spent below 3.0 mmol/L (54 mg/dL) - classified as "Level 2 hypoglycemia" under the international CGM consensus, representing clinically significant, dangerous hypoglycemia.
Low (3-3.8) (%) Low. Percentage of time spent between 3.0-3.8 mmol/L (54-69 mg/dL) - "Level 1 hypoglycemia," a glucose alert level below target but above the more severe Very Low threshold.
High (10.1-13.9) (%) High. Percentage of time spent between 10.1-13.9 mmol/L (181-250 mg/dL) - "Level 1 hyperglycemia," above target but below the more severe Very High threshold.
V.High (>13.9) (%) Very High. Percentage of time spent above 13.9 mmol/L (250 mg/dL) - "Level 2 hyperglycemia," representing clinically significant, severe hyperglycemia.
