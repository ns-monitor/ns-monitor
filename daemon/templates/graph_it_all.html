Welcome to Graph-it-All

What is this workbench for?

This is an open exploratory sandbox designed to let you freely cross-examine, correlate, and fold any Nightscout metrics across your entire history.

A candid heads-up:

Because you have total freedom to map any metric to any axis at any time grain, it is entirely possible to create completely clinically meaningless graphs (like Total Daily Dose vs Time of Day or Basal Insulin vs Day of Month) alongside genuinely insightful ones (like Average Glucose vs Coefficient of Variation, or Insulin Sensitivity by Day of Week).

High-speed guarded database engine:

Every time you click Update Graph, your request is compiled into a single optimized analytical SQL query. All aggregations run directly in PostgreSQL under a guarded 6,000ms query budget statement. Queries typically return in a few dozen milliseconds, giving you instant answers without overloading the database.

Getting started:

Pick a time grain or periodic cycle fold in Box 1, choose metrics for your X and Y axes in Box 2, select a statistical curve fit in Box 3, and hit Update Graph!

Freely cross-examine, correlate, and fold Nightscout metrics across any timescale or periodic cycle — featuring multi-grain aggregations, dual-axis overlays, closed-form statistical curve fits, and confidence corridors.

1. Grain & Format

Grain & Format Guide

Observation Grain:

Determines how raw readings are bucketed into individual data points before plotting: from high-resolution 5-minute intervals up to monthly summaries.

Chart Format:

Scatter: Best for observing correlation, clusters, and spread between two variables.
Line: Ideal for seeing progression across continuous time or cycles.
Bar: Great for discrete comparisons across days or categories.

Cycle Fold (Periodicity):

Instead of plotting one long continuous timeline, cycle folds compress and overlay your history onto repeating biological or calendar rhythms:

  • Timeline: Standard chronological order.
  • 24h Diurnal: Folds all selected days into a single 24-hour day (00:00–23:00) to reveal daily circadian patterns.
  • 7d Week: Overlays your data by day of the week (Monday through Sunday) to uncover weekend vs weekday habits.
  • 12m Annual: Folds multi-year history into 12 calendar months to highlight seasonal trends.
  • Custom X-d: Folds data into an arbitrary X-day cycle (e.g. 3-day pump pod sites, 10-day CGM sensor changes, or 28-day hormonal cycles) anchored to a specific reference date.

Day of Week Filter:

Isolates specific days of the week (e.g. weekdays vs weekends) so you can eliminate routine disruptions from your analysis.

2. Axis Roles

Axis Roles Guide

X Axis (Abscissa):

The independent variable plotted along the horizontal axis. In standard timeline mode, you can pick any metric (e.g., Carbs, Time, Sensor Glucose). When a Cycle Fold is active, the X axis automatically locks to the cycle's time buckets (e.g. Mon–Sun or Jan–Dec).

Y Axis (Primary Left Ordinate):

The primary dependent variable plotted on the left vertical axis (e.g. Total Daily Dose, Mean Glucose). All trend fitting and confidence corridors in Box 3 are calculated against this primary Y axis.

Secondary Y₂ Axis (Right Ordinate):

Allows you to overlay an independent second metric with its own scale on the right edge of the chart (e.g., plotting Daily Carbs on Y₁ and Total Insulin on Y₂). Use the <-> Swap Y₁ ⇄ Y₂ button to swap which metric receives statistical trend modeling!

Aggregation Functions:

When multiple readings exist in a time grain (e.g. dozens of CGM readings in a single day), this controls how they combine: Average, Sum, Median, Spread (SD, CV%, IQR), or Percentiles.

3. Models & Action

Statistical Models Guide

Trend Fit Model:

Mathematically fits a curve through your primary observations to uncover underlying relationships:

  • Linear (y = mx + c): Checks for a direct, proportional relationship.
  • Quadratic (y = ax² + bx + c): Captures U-shaped or inverted curves (e.g., diminishing returns).
  • Exponential / Power Law: Models non-linear compound changes.
  • LOESS Smoothing: Non-parametric local regression that smoothly bends to follow the data organically without imposing an equation.

Confidence vs Prediction Corridors:

  • 95% Mean Confidence Corridor: Shows where the true average relationship lies with 95% certainty. A narrow band indicates high certainty in the overall trend line.
  • 95% Prediction Interval: Shows the corridor where 95% of individual future observations are expected to fall. It is wider because it accounts for day-to-day scatter.
  • Quantile Ribbons (IQR / AGP): Shaded percentiles (such as the standard 25th–75th interquartile range) calculated directly from observation bins.

Note: Statistical models operate strictly on the Primary Y (Left) Axis. Use the <-> Swap Y₁ ⇄ Y₂ button if you want to model your secondary metric.
